### CARE Scribe — a scribe that follows the clinician

**What I’m proud of:** I tried to understand the workflow and practical constraints before settling on a direction. The work considered setup, maintenance, connectivity, and how a solution could fit into the way clinicians already work.

**Important context:** This was a design direction I proposed as part of my contribution to an open-source project. Don't describe the proposal as a launched CARE feature or imply that it has been adopted.

### Guideline Agent — accessibility guidance in pull requests

**What it is:** An open-source, learning-first AI reviewer for GitHub pull requests that looks for WCAG accessibility issues. It combines deterministic checks from eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y with AI-assisted review and returns contextual findings and suggested fixes.

**My role:** Independent design and engineering project.

**What I’m proud of:** The idea grew from a pattern I noticed while working on accessibility: issues such as color contrast, inaccessible custom icons, and missing alternative text can be fixed individually, but similar problems can reappear as a product and its contributors evolve. I wanted project-specific guidance to meet contributors inside the pull-request workflow instead of expecting everyone to remember every guideline.

**Limitations:** The current project is a read-only reviewer. It does not modify files, create commits, push code, or apply automatic fixes. Its findings are advisory and should be reviewed.

**GitHub:** https://github.com/navajyoth-puthalath13/Guideline-Agent

## How I work

### Start with the problem

I enjoy working on complex problems. I like understanding the context, observing what feels easy or frustrating, and asking why something works the way it does before jumping into a screen or feature.

### Think in systems

A product is more than an isolated interface. It sits within workflows, existing tools, conventions, and people's mental models. I look for how those pieces connect and how the experience can fit into them without adding unnecessary friction.

### Design with implementation in mind

Code isn't about becoming an engineer for me; it's a way to become a sharper designer. It helps me move beyond what is easy to mock up, understand real product constraints, prototype interactions, and communicate with engineers in the same language.

I use code throughout the process—from quick demos and experiments to components and end-to-end prototypes. I’m comfortable exploring a codebase and connecting design decisions to how something is built.

### Accessibility belongs in the workflow

I see accessibility as part of product quality, not a final checklist. My work has included improving color contrast, adding accessible names to custom SVG icons, adding meaningful alternative text, and exploring ways to help teams catch recurring issues earlier.

### Keep iterating

I don’t expect every project to have all the answers at the start. I like testing an idea, finding what doesn’t work, revisiting decisions, and improving it one iteration at a time.
